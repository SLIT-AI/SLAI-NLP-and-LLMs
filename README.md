# Natural Language Processing and Large Language Models

**Ph.D. Course · Autumn 2026 · Shenzhen Loop Area Institute**

[Visit the Course Website](https://slit-ai.github.io/SLAI-NLP-and-LLMs/)

## About the Course

This course introduces the foundations, methods, and research frontiers of natural language processing and large language models. It connects core concepts—from tokenization, representation learning, and Transformer architectures—to modern topics including pretraining, alignment, retrieval-augmented generation, reasoning, agents, and trustworthy evaluation.

Classes combine conceptual lectures, discussions of representative research, practical demonstrations, and project-based learning. Students will learn not only how modern language models work, but also how to examine their limitations and design meaningful experiments around them.

## What You Will Learn

By the end of the course, students should be able to:

- Explain the principles behind modern NLP systems and foundation models.
- Analyze pretraining, scaling, instruction tuning, preference alignment, retrieval, reasoning, and agent frameworks.
- Implement, adapt, and evaluate language models using contemporary tools and parameter-efficient methods.
- Critically assess model capabilities, benchmarks, reliability, safety, and ethical considerations.
- Design and conduct an independent research project in NLP, LLMs, multimodal foundation models, or trustworthy AI.

## Course Topics

The course progresses through five connected areas:

1. Tokens, representations, and embeddings
2. Neural language models, attention, and Transformers
3. Pretraining, scaling, fine-tuning, and alignment
4. Retrieval-augmented generation, agents, and tool use
5. Reasoning, evaluation, reliability, and emerging research directions

A detailed 15-week roadmap is available on the [course website](https://slit-ai.github.io/SLAI-NLP-and-LLMs/#schedule).

## Course Format and Assessment

Each week combines two sessions on concepts, theory, and representative open models with one session dedicated to individual practice, observation, and discussion.

- **Final Group Project:** 75%
- **In-class Engagement:** 25%

In-class engagement includes attendance, demonstrations, short tasks, questions, and discussion. Project submission alone is not sufficient for the highest grade.

## Prerequisites

Students should be proficient in Python and have a foundation in linear algebra, probability, and machine learning. Previous experience with PyTorch or TensorFlow is strongly recommended.
